## 1. Organisation Overview

Community Development Company Nesting (CDCN) is a Scottish charitable organisation and company limited by guarantee, based in South Nesting, Shetland. Governed by OSCR (Office of the Scottish Charity Regulator) and Companies House Scotland, CDCN serves the communities of South Nesting, Girlsta, Wadbister, and the surrounding rural area.

CDCN was established to save and repurpose the former South Nesting Primary School ("The Aald Skül") for community benefit. Since its formation, the organisation has developed a diverse portfolio of community assets and services, including the Nesting Scrapstore, community growing spaces (Polycrubs), the Nesting Gym, Bird Hide facilities, and the Business Studio. These assets address local needs identified through extensive community consultation, including the *Nesting, Girlsta and Wadbister Community Action Plan 2024–2034*.

CDCN has a demonstrated track record of securing and managing funding from multiple sources, including Highlands and Islands Enterprise (HIE), Shetland Islands Council (SIC), the Scottish Land Fund, CARES Scotland, the Shetland Community Benefit Fund, and various lottery distributors. The organisation is governed by a volunteer Board of Directors and employs a small team including a Development Officer and facility staff. ## 6. Value for Money / In-Kind Contribution

### Commercial Cost Estimation for the CDCN Agent System

CDCN wishes to draw funders' attention to a significant in-kind contribution that substantially enhances the value for money of this funding request: the **CDCN Agent system**, an AI-powered administrative assistant platform developed specifically to support the organisation's governance, compliance, and operational activities.

Had this system been commissioned from a commercial software development agency, the costs would have been considerable. The following breakdown provides a realistic estimate of what custom development of such a system would require:

#### System Capabilities

The CDCN Agent system provides the following functionality:

- **Document search and archive** — indexing and retrieval across organisational records, minutes, policies, and correspondence
- **Document drafting** — automated preparation of board minutes, policy documents, funding applications, trustee reports, and other governance materials
- **Calendar and deadline tracking** — monitoring of statutory deadlines (OSCR returns, filing requirements), grant reporting dates, and action point due dates
- **Action point tracking** — extraction, assignment, and persistence of action items from meeting minutes, with escalation workflows
- **Funding pipeline monitoring** — Kanban-board style tracking of funding applications by status, funder, amount, and probability
- **Overnight and automated review capabilities** — batch processing of documents against organisational templates, style guides, and compliance checklists

#### Commercial Development Cost Breakdown

A system of this scope, built to production quality with appropriate security, testing, and documentation, would typically require a 6–9 month development programme involving the following roles:

| Role | Day Rate | Duration | Estimated Cost |
|------|----------|----------|----------------|
| Project Manager / Senior Developer | £550–£650 | ~120 days (6 months, part-time PM + hands-on dev) | £66,000 – £78,000 |
| Full-Stack Developer | £400–£500 | ~120 days (backend API, database, integration work) | £48,000 – £60,000 |
| UI/UX Designer | £350–£450 | ~40 days (interface design, workflow visualisation) | £14,000 – £18,000 |
| QA / Testing Specialist | £300–£400 | ~30 days (test planning, execution, regression) | £9,000 – £12,000 |
| DevOps / Infrastructure | £350–£450 | ~15 days (server setup, deployment pipelines, security hardening) | £5,250 – £6,750 |
| Project Management Overhead | — | Contingency for meetings, documentation, stakeholder liaison | £4,000 – £6,000 |
| **Subtotal** | | | **£146,250 – £180,750** |
| **Contingency (10–15%)** | | | **£14,600 – £27,100** |
| **Total Commercial Estimate** | | | **£160,850 – £207,850** |

Based on a more conservative, streamlined approach using a leaner team structure over 5–6 months, a realistic **minimum commercial cost estimate falls in the range of £80,000 – £150,000**, depending on scope refinement and vendor selection.

#### Actual Development Costs (In-Kind Contribution)

The CDCN Agent system was instead developed through:

- **Volunteer technical development time:** approximately **100+ hours** of skilled volunteer labour, including system architecture, prompt engineering, knowledge-base construction, workflow design, and iterative testing
- **Direct costs:** approximately **£150** in direct expenditure (API subscriptions, minimal cloud resources during development, domain costs)

This represents an **in-kind contribution valued at approximately £80,000–£150,000** (using the conservative commercial estimate) that has already been invested in CDCN's organisational capacity before any external funding for this project is drawn upon.

#### Value-for-Money Implications for Funders

This in-kind contribution delivers tangible benefits to funders:

1. **Reduced administrative overheads:** The system automates routine drafting, formatting, and compliance-checking tasks, meaning a higher proportion of funded staff time can be directed to frontline delivery rather than administration.

2. **Improved reporting quality:** Standardised templates, automatic deadline tracking, and pre-submission reviews reduce errors in funder reports and ensure timely submission of deliverables.

3. **Enhanced organisational resilience:** By preserving institutional memory in a searchable, structured format, the system mitigates risks associated with personnel changes — a common challenge for small community organisations.

4. **Demonstrated resourcefulness:** The successful development of this capability through volunteer effort and minimal expenditure demonstrates CDCN's ability to maximise the impact of every pound invested.

5. **Scalable infrastructure:** The system is designed to handle increased workload as CDCN's portfolio of projects grows, meaning the initial investment continues to yield returns without proportional cost increases.

#### Community Resourcefulness

This approach reflects a core strength of CDCN and the South Nesting community: the ability to leverage skills, creativity, and voluntary effort to achieve outcomes that would otherwise require substantial external investment. It is consistent with the organisation's track record of delivering capital projects (such as the Scrapstore expansion, Polycrub installations, and solar PV/battery systems) through a combination of grant funding, volunteer labour, and careful stewardship of resources.

Funders supporting CDCN are therefore benefiting from a mature, functional administrative platform that would typically represent a five-to-six-figure capital investment — an investment the community has effectively made on itself before approaching external funders for project-specific support.
