## 2. Why This Matters

This investment addresses four interconnected strategic priorities for CDCN:

### 2.1 Data Security and Regulatory Compliance

CDCN processes sensitive organisational documents including **funding applications**, **trustee reports**, **staff personnel files**, **financial statements**, and **community consultation data** containing personal information. Under the *Data (Use and Access) Act 2025* and GDPR obligations, transmitting this material to external AI service providers creates ongoing compliance risk and requires repeated data protection impact assessments. Local hosting eliminates this exposure entirely.

### 2.2 Operational Efficiency for Volunteer Directors

The Board of Directors is entirely volunteer-run at governance level, supported by part-time staff. Administrative burdens — drafting minutes, preparing funding applications, searching historical records, formatting board papers — consume significant volunteer hours that could otherwise be directed towards community engagement and project delivery. A capable local AI system can reduce routine document-processing tasks from **hours to minutes**, directly supporting director wellbeing and sustainability.

### 2.3 Institutional Memory Preservation

CDCN has experienced changes in board membership and personnel over its operational lifetime. Critical knowledge regarding past decisions, funder relationships, project history, and organisational learning risks being lost during transitions. The CDCN Agent is explicitly designed to **preserve institutional memory** by maintaining searchable access to indexed archives of minutes, policies, funding applications, and correspondence — but only if it can process that volume of data effectively.

### 2.4 Future-Proofing Organisational Capacity

As CDCN's portfolio of community assets grows (Scrapstore, Growing Spaces/Polycrubs, Nesting Gym, Bird Hide, South Nesting Public Hall support, Heritage Trail development), the administrative complexity scales accordingly. Investing in local AI infrastructure now positions the organisation to handle increased workload without proportional increases in administrative cost or volunteer burnout.

### Current Limitations

| Limitation | Impact on Operations |
|------------|---------------------|

| Response latency varies (3–15 seconds typical) | Disrupts workflow for real-time meeting assistance |
| No capability for local fine-tuning or custom model adaptation | Cannot develop specialised knowledge of Shetland-specific funding landscape or dialect |
| Ongoing subscription costs unpredictable | Budgeting difficulty; costs scale with usage volume |
| Internet outage = no AI assistance | During power or connectivity issues (not uncommon in rural Shetland), system unavailable |

## 9. Background

### 9.1 Organisational Context

Community Development Company Nesting (CDCN) is a Scottish charitable organisation and company limited by guarantee (SC048164), based at **The Aald Skül** (the former South Nesting Primary School) in South Nesting, Shetland. Founded in December 2016 to prevent the disposal of this community asset, CDCN has since developed a diverse portfolio of community facilities and projects including:

- **Nesting Scrapstore** — salvage materials enterprise providing affordable materials to the community
- **Growing Spaces / Polycrubs** — community growing plots serving over 30 households
- **Nesting Community Gym** — fitness facility with 500+ active members
- **Bird Hide** — wildlife observation facility completed 2025
- **South Nesting Community Heritage Trail** — tourism and heritage project (HIE-funded, in development)
- **Aald Skül community hub** — multi-use venue housing enterprises, workshops, and gathering spaces

### 9.2 The CDCN Agent Initiative

During 2025–2026, CDCN began developing the **CDCN Agent** — an AI-assisted administrative support system designed to address the challenge of managing increasing organisational complexity with limited volunteer and part-time staff resources. The initiative is governed by **Policy SPEC-2025-001 (CDCN Artificial Intelligence and Ethics Policy)**, adopted by the Board, which establishes principles including:

- Human-on-the-Loop (HOTL) oversight for all AI-generated outputs
- Prohibition against AI making independent decisions affecting community members
- Data sovereignty requirements keeping sensitive information within CDCN control
- Anti-bias mandates preventing discriminatory outcomes
- Alignment with charitable objectives and Community Action Plan 2024–2034

### 9.3 Strategic Alignment

This hardware upgrade directly supports multiple themes from the *Nesting, Girlsta and Wadbister Community Action Plan 2024–2034*:

| Action Plan Theme | How This Project Contributes |
|------------------|------------------------------|
| **Theme 1: A Natural Place with a Sustainable Environment** | Reduces reliance on energy-intensive cloud data centres; leverages existing solar PV installation at Aald Skül |
| **Theme 3: A Place Where People Come Together** | Frees volunteer time for face-to-face community engagement rather than administrative tasks |
| **Theme 4: A Connected Place** | Improves internal communication and information sharing across dispersed rural community |
| **Cross-cutting: Organisational Sustainability** | Builds capacity to manage growing portfolio without proportional staff cost increases |

### 9.4 Governance and Accountability

This project will operate under the following governance framework:

- **Board oversight:** Progress reports to each scheduled Board meeting (fortnightly)
- **Financial accountability:** Separate cost code for tracking; report to Treasurer monthly
- **Data protection:** Registered processing activity under CDCN's Data Protection framework; DPIA completed prior to processing personal data
- **AI Ethics compliance:** All usage logged per Policy SPEC-2025-001; annual review of outputs and effectiveness
- **Evaluation:** Success metrics include response time benchmarks, user satisfaction surveys, documented hours saved, and qualitative assessment of output quality
