## Objectives and Activities

### The Aald Skül

Community Development Company Nesting (CDCN) was formed in December 2016 by local residents who came together to save the former South Nesting Primary School — the Aald Skül — from disposal by Shetland Islands Council (SIC). The group successfully secured the building for community benefit and incorporated as a company limited by guarantee on 22 February 2018 (Company Number 589570). CDCN was registered as a Scottish charity on 27 February 2018 (SC048164).

The Aald Skül now serves as a community hub, providing facilities including the Nesting Scrapstore, the Nesting Gym, business units, growing spaces, and venues for community groups and activities. The building continues to be a focal point for community life in South Nesting and the wider area.

### Community Ideas

CDCN operates a membership model open to all residents of the South Nesting area, ensuring that the organisation remains community-led and responsive to local needs. During 2024, CDCN conducted a comprehensive community survey and stakeholder consultation to inform the development of the *Nesting, Girlsta & Wadbister Community Action Plan 2024–2034*. This plan was completed and published in September 2024 following extensive community engagement. It identifies key priorities including affordable housing, childcare provision, energy efficiency, improved transport infrastructure, and enhanced community facilities. CDCN remains committed to progressing these priorities through partnership working and community-led development.

## Achievements and Performance

### Community Action Plan and Local Place Planning

The *Nesting, Girlsta and Wadbister Community Action Plan 2024–2034* has guided CDCN's work throughout the reporting period. The plan sets out clear priorities across five themes: A Natural Place with a Sustainable Environment; A Place with Things to do and Places to Do Them; A Place Where People Come Together; A Connected Place; and A Healthy Place.

During 2025/26, CDCN participated in meetings regarding the development of a Local Place Plan informed by the Community Action Plan. A meeting was held with David Wood from Planning Aid Scotland in March 2026 to discuss opportunities for developing a Local Place Plan, which would provide the community with a stronger voice in the planning system and help shape future development in the area.

### Development Officer Post

A new Development Officer commenced work at the end of July 2025, funded for 25 hours per week. Ensuring continuity and an effective handover required considerable planning by the board and staff.

In March 2026, CDCN held a funding meeting with Highlands and Islands Enterprise (HIE) — attended by Fiona Stirling (HIE), Ellis Keith and Mark Ratter — to discuss grant funding for the Development Officer position for a further 12 months from April 2026 to March 2027. The grant application was subsequently received and submitted to HIE. A final allocation of **£6,175** from the current DO funding period was received before the end of March 2026.

Recruitment for the continuing DO post progressed during the year, with advertising planned for April 2026, interviews in May 2026, and a target start date of mid-June 2026. Mark Ratter indicated willingness to continue in the role part-time if required, with HIE content with this arrangement as a backup option.

### Growing Spaces / Polycrubs / Allotments

Rental of the polycrubs continued throughout the year, with strong demand and a waiting list maintained for plots. Groundworks for access paths and road drains were completed earlier in the project by Maurice Sutherland. Nort Energy constructed the Polycrubs on site, and Jim Sandison completed fencing and windbreaks. Electrical connection to the main Polycrub was established.

### Bird Hide

Maintenance work was undertaken at the bird hide during the year, including repairs to the handrail and weeding of access paths. The site remains a valued community asset for wildlife observation and enjoyment of the local environment.

### Nesting Scrapstore

The Scrapstore continued operations from its premises at the Aald Skül, providing salvage materials and resources for community projects and creative reuse. Plant sales generated approximately **£200** during the reporting period. The store remained popular with approximately eight core volunteers (with mid to high regularity of volunteering) plus several more occasional volunteers supporting its day-to-day running.

A significant milestone was reached in March 2026 with the completion of the CLLD-funded improvements project. George Robertsons installed a washing machine and tumble dryer at the Scrap Store on 17 March 2026, including fitting an outside vent for the dryer. A microwave and fridge procured under the same grant were placed in the Office. Planning also progressed for energy-efficient LED lighting within the Scrapstore building.

Discussions took place regarding potential income generation through community lotteries, with enquiries made to SIC Legal Service regarding licensing requirements under the Gambling Act.

### Nesting Gym

The gym continued to operate throughout the year, providing fitness facilities for local residents. In December 2025, an assault bike was ordered for the gym. Discussions also took place regarding heating options for the facility.

A blocked drain in the gym toilet was identified on 15 March 2026, with Ellis Keith attending on 16 March 2026 to clear the blockage and resolve the issue before any flooding risk materialised. The drainage system was confirmed to be sound following this intervention.

### Business Studio

The Business Studio continued to provide rental space for sole traders and small businesses. Occupancy rates remained stable during the reporting period. The future use of the Business Suite was discussed during the year, with the space being used over winter by the Nesting Local History Group for sorting and organising archives and materials.

### Foodbank and Community Resilience

Funding was secured from the SSEN Community Resilience Fund for the acquisition of a portable generator to support the community during power outages. The South Nesting Public Hall is being developed as a resilience hub for emergency situations.

### Community Heritage Trail App

Andrew Foyle presented an update on the CDCN heritage trail app, which he has been continuing to develop as a potential revenue stream. The app allows users to access guided heritage trails unlocked via QR codes at trail sites. The proposed monetisation model includes:

- Free initial Nesting rollout with a donation button
- Expansion to other areas of Shetland as paid trail packs (approximately £1.99–£2.99 per pack)
- Potential bulk access arrangements with Lerwick Port Authority and cruise ship operators
- Revenue sharing with heritage and history groups contributing content
- Platform fees retained by Apple (approximately 30%) and Android (approximately 15%), with remaining revenue split between CDCN and content contributors

The app platform was confirmed as already built, and directors expressed support for the concept.

### Housing Initiatives

The board continued to explore options for affordable housing in the area throughout the reporting period. In September 2025, Brydon Sinclair reported an informative meeting with a consultant available through the Scottish Community Business Fund (SCBF), gathering information about processes and requirements for housing projects going forward.

Directors noted that the Rural and Islands Housing Fund can only be applied for once, meaning considerable preliminary research work — including a Housing Needs Assessment — would need to be completed beforehand. The board agreed to seek other sources of funding for preliminary data gathering. An initial enquiry was sent to SIC Planning Service regarding whether land behind the Aald Skül could be an option for new builds.

### Childcare

CDCN supported the Nesting Childcare Working Group throughout the year. Two potential funding sources were identified: the Reactive Fund from Scottish Community Business Fund (SCBF) and the Community Local Led Development (CLLD) programme. The board agreed to explore options with the lead contacts of the Nesting Childcare Working Group on how best to formalise the group's structure before proceeding with funding applications to commission a feasibility and options study.

### Energy Efficiency and Solar PV Installation

Significant progress was made on energy efficiency improvements during the year. CDCN secured funding from CARES Scotland and the Shetland Community Benefit Fund for the installation of solar panels and battery storage at the Aald Skül. Nordri commissioned the DC side of the installation, with AC commissioning scheduled for early October 2025. The installation progressed during the year, reducing energy costs and contributing to the organisation's sustainability objectives.

An application for a feed-in tariff was in preparation, though progress was dependent on resolving ongoing billing issues with Octopus Energy regarding meter registration. Mandy pursued correspondence with Octopus Energy throughout the latter part of the reporting period, with photographic evidence of the meter supplied and slow but positive progress reported.

Additional electrical works were completed during the year, including a new connection point becoming operational. Quotes were received for further efficiency works to be included in CLLD funding applications, covering LED lighting, heaters connected to invertors (allowing buildings to stay lit and heated during power cuts), and a generator change-over switch.

### Active Travel and Infrastructure

In March 2026, Jennie Bradley tabled a document relating to a *Nesting Active Travel Audit* produced by an external organisation on behalf of SIC. Directors discussed the audit findings and agreed a response supporting:

- Improved footpaths along the South Nesting loop road, with extension of the assessed scope beyond the school to shop section
- Opposition to streetlight installation given the rural character and community value placed on dark skies
- No requirement for separate cycling infrastructure beyond adequate footpaths serving pedestrians and young cyclists
- Support for extension of reduced speed limits along relevant road sections
- Noted concern that the audit should have involved community engagement at time of conduct rather than retrospective feedback
