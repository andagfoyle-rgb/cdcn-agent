# Trustee Report — 2025/26

## Objectives and Activities

### The Aald Skül

Community Development Company Nesting (CDCN) was founded in December 2016 by local residents who came together to save the former South Nesting Primary School — the Aald Skül — from disposal by Shetland Islands Council. The group successfully secured the building for community benefit and incorporated as a company limited by guarantee on 22 February 2018 (Company Number 589570). CDCN registered as a Scottish charity on 27 February 2018 (Charity Number SC048164).

The Aald Skül now serves as a community hub providing facilities, services, and activities for the benefit of residents in South Nesting and the wider area. CDCN continues to manage and develop the building in response to community needs, with the aim of creating a sustainable asset that supports local wellbeing, economic activity, and social connection.

### Community Ideas

CDCN remains committed to a community-led approach to development. The organisation operates a membership model, giving local residents a direct voice in shaping priorities and activities. The Community Action Plan for Nesting, Girlsta and Wadbister, published in September 2024, provides a clear framework for CDCN's work, with priority themes including housing, childcare, and community cohesion.

Throughout the year, CDCN has continued to engage with members, volunteers, and partner organisations to identify opportunities and respond to emerging needs. The board remains focused on ensuring that CDCN's work reflects the priorities identified by the community.

## Achievements and Performance

### Development Officer Position

CDCN experienced a transition in the Development Officer role during the year. Following the departure of the previous post-holder, a new Development Officer was appointed and commenced work at the end of July 2025. The new role is funded for 25 hours per week. Ensuring continuity and an effective handover required considerable planning by the board and staff. In March 2026, CDCN held a funding meeting with Highlands and Islands Enterprise (HIE) to secure grant funding for the Development Officer position for a further 12 months from April 2026 to March 2027. The grant application was submitted and approval was anticipated by March 2026.

### Scrapstore

The Scrapstore continued to operate throughout the year, providing a popular community resource for salvage, building materials, and household items. The store relies on a team of core volunteers alongside paid team leaders. During the year, the board discussed ways to improve transparency around Scrapstore finances, including a proposal to add a straightforward explanation of income and expenditure to the published annual accounts. Work also progressed on plans to improve energy efficiency in the Scrapstore building, including LED lighting and additional heating connected to the solar panel system.

### Nesting Gym

The gym at the Aald Skül continued to serve local residents throughout the year. In December 2025, a new assault bike was ordered for the gym. The board also submitted a funding bid to improve gym facilities, including new equipment, a commercial washer and dryer, and electrical works. A meeting was scheduled with HIE for early March 2026 to discuss funding opportunities for the gym project. Board members also discussed the need to resolve administrative matters relating to the gym's social media presence and ensure appropriate access for ongoing management.

### Business Studio

The Business Studio was vacated by its previous users earlier in the year. During the winter months, the Nesting Local History Group made use of the space to catalogue and organise records stored at the Aald Skül. The board continues to explore options for future use of the Business Studio to generate income and support local enterprise.

### Childcare

Addressing the shortage of childcare provision in the area remains a priority identified in the Community Action Plan. During the year, CDCN continued to seek funding for a childcare feasibility study and options appraisal. Two potential funding sources were identified: the Scottish Community Banking Fund (SCBF) Reactive Fund and the Community Led Local Development (CLLD) programme. The board also discussed the need to formalise the structure of the Nesting Childcare Working Group before proceeding with funding applications.
