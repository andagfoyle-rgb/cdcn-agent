# Trustee Report — 2025–26

## Objectives and Activities

### The Aald Skül

Community Development Company Nesting (CDCN) was founded in December 2016 by local residents seeking to save the former South Nesting Primary School, known locally as the Aald Skül, from disposal by Shetland Islands Council. The building was identified as a vital community asset that could be repurposed to serve the social, economic, and environmental needs of the South Nesting area.

CDCN was incorporated as a company limited by guarantee on 22 February 2018 (Company Number 589570) and registered as a Scottish charity on 27 February 2018 (SC048164). The charity is regulated by OSCR (Office of the Scottish Charity Regulator) and governed by a Board of Directors elected from the membership. The Aald Skül remains CDCN's primary asset and forms the hub around which community development activities are organised.

### Community Ideas

CDCN operates a membership model open to all residents of the South Nesting area, ensuring that the organisation remains community-led and responsive to local needs. During 2024, CDCN conducted a comprehensive community survey and stakeholder consultation to inform the development of the Nesting, Girlsta & Wadbister Community Action Plan 2024–2034. This plan, produced with funding from Community Led Local Development (CLLD), identifies key priorities including affordable housing, childcare provision, energy efficiency, and improved community facilities. CDCN remains committed to progressing these priorities through partnership working and community-led development.

### Energy Efficiency and Solar PV

Significant progress was made on energy efficiency improvements during the reporting period. Funding was secured from CARES Scotland and Shetland Community Benefit Fund for the installation of solar PV panels and battery storage at the Aald Skül. Installation works proceeded, with the system expected to reduce energy costs and provide feed-in tariff income. The board discussed applying for the feed-in tariff following the electricity bill review. An issue regarding a backdated electricity bill from Octopus was also addressed.

### Community Resilience

CDCN received funding from the SSEN Community Resilience Fund to support the development of South Nesting Public Hall as a resilience hub, including the provision of a portable generator to support the community during power outages. The project reflects CDCN's commitment to community safety and preparedness.

### Community Heritage Trail

A funding application was submitted to Highlands and Islands Enterprise for the South Nesting Community Heritage Trail project. The application requested £15,000 to support signage, lighting, and access pathways at heritage sites including Gletness and Ling Ness. The project aims to increase visitor numbers and promote local heritage. The application outcome was awaited at the end of the reporting period.

### South Nesting Public Hall

CDCN continued to support South Nesting Public Hall, with rental and maintenance arrangements in place. Funding was identified for electrical switchboard improvements. The hall serves as an important venue for community events and activities.

### Housing Initiatives

Affordable housing was identified in the Community Action Plan as a priority theme. CDCN continued to explore the Rural Islands Housing Fund and other opportunities to address local housing needs. A housing needs assessment was proposed as part of wider community consultation work.

### Development Officer Post

The Development Officer post, funded by Highlands and Islands Enterprise, remained central to CDCN's capacity to deliver projects. The current funding agreement was due to conclude in July 2026. A meeting was held with HIE on 6 March 2026 regarding continuation funding for a further 12 months from April 2026 to March 2027. A final allocation of £6,175 was confirmed. Discussions took place regarding retaining the Development Officer on a consultancy basis under the new funding arrangement. A case study and photographer visit from HIE were arranged to profile CDCN's work.

### Childcare

Addressing the shortage of childcare provision was identified as a key community priority. CDCN continued to investigate options for childcare delivery, with engagement with the Parent Council and other stakeholders. Correspondence was ongoing regarding CDCN's attendance at Parent Council meetings.

### Up Helly Aa

CDCN supported the Nesting and Girlsta Up Helly Aa, with discussions recorded regarding the expansion of the galley burning site at Aald Skül.

### Community Defibrillator

Fundraising for a community defibrillator progressed during the year. Contact was made with Scottish Sea Farms and other potential funders.
