# Trustee Report — 2025/26

## Objectives and Activities

### The Aald Skül

Community Development Company Nesting (CDCN) was founded in December 2016 by local residents who came together to save the former South Nesting Primary School — the Aald Skül — from disposal by Shetland Islands Council (SIC). The group successfully secured the building for community benefit and incorporated as a company limited by guarantee on 22 February 2018 (Company Number 589570). CDCN was registered as a Scottish charity on 27 February 2018 (SC048164).

The Aald Skül now serves as a community hub, providing facilities and services that support local residents and community groups. The building hosts a range of activities and tenants, contributing to the social and economic wellbeing of South Nesting and the wider area.

### Community Ideas

CDCN operates a membership model, enabling local residents to participate in decision-making and shape the organisation's priorities. The Community Action Plan for Nesting, Girlsta and Wadbister was completed and published in September 2024 following extensive community consultation. This plan guides CDCN's work programme and informs discussions with external partners and funders. Priority themes identified include housing and childcare, which remain key long-term issues for the area.

CDCN remains committed to community-led development, responding to local needs and supporting initiatives brought forward by residents and community groups.

## Achievements and Performance

### Community Action Plan

The Nesting, Girlsta and Wadbister Community Action Plan 2024–2034 was published in September 2024. The plan identifies key priorities including housing, childcare, tourism infrastructure, and community cohesion. Throughout 2025/26, CDCN has used the Action Plan to guide its work and to support discussions with funders and partners. CDCN has also been involved in discussions regarding the development of a Local Place Plan informed by the Community Action Plan.

### Development Officer Post

A new Development Officer commenced work at the end of July 2025, funded for 25 hours per week. Ensuring continuity and an effective handover required considerable planning by the board and staff. In March 2026, CDCN held a funding meeting with Highlands and Islands Enterprise (HIE) — attended by Fiona Stirling (HIE), Ellis Keith and Mark Ratter — to discuss grant funding for the Development Officer position for a further 12 months from April 2026 to March 2027. The grant application was subsequently received by CDCN, with Mark Ratter undertaking completion and submission.

The board continued to explore options for affordable housing in the area. In September 2025, the board discussed the Rural and Islands Housing Fund, noting that it can only be applied for once, and that preliminary research work — including a Housing Needs Assessment — would need to be completed beforehand. The board agreed to seek other funding sources for preliminary data gathering. Discussions also took place regarding land availability for potential housing projects. ### Childcare

CDCN supported the Nesting Childcare Working Group throughout the year. Two potential funding sources were identified: the Reactive Fund from Scottish Community Business Fund (SCBF) and the Community Local Led Development (CLLD) programme. The board agreed to explore options with the lead contacts of the Nesting Childcare Working Group on how best to formalise the group's structure before proceeding with funding applications to commission a feasibility and options study.
