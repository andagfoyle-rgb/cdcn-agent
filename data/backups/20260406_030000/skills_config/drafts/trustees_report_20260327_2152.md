# Trustee Report — 2025–26

## Objectives and Activities

### The Aald Skül

Community Development Company Nesting (CDCN) was formed in December 2016 by local residents following the decision by Shetland Islands Council to declare the former South Nesting Primary School surplus to requirements. The community came together to save the building, known locally as the Aald Skül, from disposal and to develop it as a community asset.

CDCN was incorporated as a company limited by guarantee on 22 February 2018 (Company Number 589570) and registered as a Scottish charity on 27 February 2018 (SC048164). The organisation is governed by a Board of Directors and regulated by the Office of the Scottish Charity Regulator (OSCR) and Companies House Scotland.

The Aald Skül remains CDCN's primary community asset, providing space for a range of activities and services that support community wellbeing, economic development, and social connection in South Nesting and the surrounding area.

### Community Ideas

CDCN operates a community membership model and remains committed to community-led development. During 2025–26, the Board continued to be guided by the Nesting, Girlsta & Wadbister Community Action Plan 2024–2034, which was developed following community consultation and sets out local priorities for the decade ahead.

The Community Action Plan identifies key themes including housing, transport, childcare, and environmental sustainability. CDCN's work programme during the year reflected these priorities, with projects focused on growing spaces, energy efficiency, and support for community facilities.

## Achievements and Performance

### Growing Spaces and Polycrubs

The Growing Spaces project continued to provide community growing opportunities at the Aald Skül site. Polycrub rentals remained in demand throughout the year, with a waiting list maintained and prospective renters contacted as plots became available. The Board discussed signage for the Growing Spaces project, with Mark Ratter progressing this work.

### Nesting Scrapstore

The Nesting Scrapstore continued operations from its premises at the Aald Skül, providing salvage materials and creative resources for community projects. Vic Thomas led the Scrapstore team as Team Leader, supported by volunteers.

Plant sales at the Scrapstore generated approximately £200 during the reporting period. The Board approved the purchase of safety equipment including first aid kits for the facility. Discussions continued regarding staff and volunteer arrangements at the Scrapstore.

The Scrapstore maintained an active presence on social media, with its Facebook page continuing to attract followers and engagement from the local community.

### Energy Efficiency and Sustainability

Significant progress was made on CDCN's energy efficiency and sustainability initiatives. Funding was secured from CARES Scotland and the Shetland Community Benefit Fund for the installation of solar photovoltaic panels and battery storage at the Aald Skül.

The Board discussed the installation of energy-efficient LED lighting within the Scrapstore building, together with heaters with inverters and solar connectivity. These improvements are intended to reduce energy costs and support the organisation's sustainability goals.

Discussions regarding the electricity supply continued throughout the year, including an application for a feed-in tariff in relation to the solar PV installation. ### Community Resilience

CDCN pursued funding from the SSEN Community Resilience Fund to support work on preparing the community hall as a resilience hub for use during power cuts and emergencies. This included discussions regarding a portable generator to provide backup power during outages.

The Board also progressed fundraising for a community defibrillator, with contact made to Scottish Sea Farms regarding potential funding support.

### Community Heritage Trail

CDCN developed a funding application for a South Nesting Community Heritage Trail, seeking £15,000 from Highlands and Islands Enterprise (HIE). The project is designed to create an engaging heritage trail showcasing South Nesting's history through signage, pathways, and accessible routes at locations including Gletness and Ling Ness.

The application was submitted in March 2026, with approval anticipated by the end of the month. If successful, the project will be delivered in partnership with Shetland Islands Council, Highlands and Islands Enterprise, and local community groups. ### Development Officer Post

The Development Officer post, funded by Highlands and Islands Enterprise, continued throughout the reporting period. The current funding arrangement runs until July 2026.

A meeting was held with Fiona Stirling of HIE on 6 March 2026 to discuss HIE grant funding for the Development Officer position for a further 12 months from April 2026 to March 2027. Mark Ratter was identified as the lead for completing the grant submission. The anticipated final allocation of £6,175 was expected to be confirmed before the end of March 2026.

The Board discussed arrangements for retaining consultancy support under the new DO funding, ensuring continuity of development work.

### South Nesting Public Hall

CDCN continued to support South Nesting Public Hall during the year. The Board discussed the replacement of electric vehicle charging points at the hall, which was listed as an action item. Links with South Nesting Hall remained an important part of CDCN's community infrastructure work.

Funding discussions included an electrical switchboard at Nesting Hall, with the Board pursuing appropriate grant sources for this work.

### Housing Initiatives

CDCN maintained its focus on addressing the shortage of affordable housing in the area, as identified in the Community Action Plan. The Board discussed housing needs and the Rural Islands Housing Fund as a potential funding source. However, constraints were noted in progressing this work during the period.

The Board continued to consider how CDCN might contribute to addressing local housing needs through its asset base and partnerships.

### Community Partnerships

CDCN worked with a range of community partners during the year. Contact was maintained with the Parent Council regarding CDCN's activities, including correspondence about meetings and attendance.

Work on the galley burning site for Nesting and Girlsta Up Helly Aa continued, with discussions regarding expansion of the site at the Aald Skül. This important cultural event remains a valued part of CDCN's support for community traditions.
